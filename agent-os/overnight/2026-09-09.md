## Needs a decision

- **Your Capital One card's register dates changed meaning on 2026-08-11, when SimpleFIN took
  over from the CSVs.** Same card, same account, `Capital One •••3448`:

  | Source                      | Rows | Sun     | Mon    | Sat |
  | --------------------------- | ---- | ------- | ------ | --- |
  | `csv:capitalone-card` 2025+ | 1217 | **275** | 128    | 256 |
  | `api:simplefin` since 8/11  | 39   | **0**   | **19** | 3   |

  The CSV's heaviest days are Sunday and Saturday, which is what card _purchases_ look like.
  The feed's heaviest day is Monday at 49% of all rows, with no Sunday at all — 8/17 alone
  carries ten. That is a weekend's purchases arriving on Monday. Under the CSV's own weekday
  distribution, drawing 39 rows with zero Sundays has probability about 4 × 10⁻⁵, so it is not
  the short window.

  The cause is not a bug in our mapping. `toParsedTransaction` already prefers
  `transacted_at` over `posted` and falls back only when there is one timestamp — and Chase
  proves the good path works: **all 19** feed rows whose two dates differ are Chase, each
  `transacted_at` one to three days before its `posted`. Capital One's SimpleFIN data reports
  the two as the same day, so we date those rows by posting because there is nothing else.

  What it costs: `transaction_date` on that card means the purchase before 8/10 and the
  posting after it. **A Sunday purchase can post into the next budget month** — this August it
  did not, only because Aug 29–30's weekend posted on Mon Aug 31; the next month-end weekend
  will not be so tidy. Insights and pay-period reporting also compare a transaction-dated past
  against a posting-dated present across that boundary.

  Options: **(a)** accept it and say so where a reader can see it — a Source/as-of note on the
  register, since `2026-09-01-1205-source-as-of-authority` already built the per-source
  currency model this would hang from; **(b)** keep importing the Capital One CSV for the card
  alongside the feed and let the cross-source matcher prefer the CSV's date — it already has
  the tolerance, and this is the only option that recovers the purchase date, at the cost of
  the manual step your workflow is meant to remove; **(c)** nothing, and remember it when a
  month-boundary weekend looks wrong.

  **Recommendation: (a).** The purchase date is not recoverable from a feed that does not send
  it, so (b) buys accuracy with exactly the manual step the SimpleFIN move was for, and only
  for one card. What is actually wrong today is that nothing tells you the column's meaning
  changed. I put the measurement in `mapping.ts` (`bfe0a1e`-adjacent commit below) so the next
  reader finds it; making it visible in the register is the part that is yours.
  — blocking: nothing. Worth deciding before the first month-end weekend that straddles.

**The detector that paid tonight was the same one as the last two nights, aimed at two new

**And the sharpest lesson is about the scans themselves.** Two of them lied tonight, in
opposite directions and for the same underlying reason — they answered a _narrower_ question
than the one I was asking:

- "Which files import this?" is not "is this dead": `parseBlockOffset` is called twenty lines
  above its own declaration.
- "Does any tracked file contain this name?" is not "does any caller use it": a spec that names
  `categoryFromBank` in the line asking for its deletion made it look live.

Both were caught by a check that costs nothing — typecheck for the first, reading the one hit
for the second. **Run the scan, then read its hits rather than its count.**
