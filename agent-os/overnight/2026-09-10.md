- **Suggest from Amazon overstates how fast you use things — by 2× for most of what it
  suggests.** The inferred rate is `totalUnits ÷ (lastOrder − firstOrder)`, exactly as
  `specs/2026-08-26-0910-supplies-worksheet/plan.md:295` specifies. That is a fencepost: the
  units bought in the _last_ order have not been used yet — the span measures how long the
  earlier orders lasted. Two orders of one item 30 days apart mean "one lasts 30 days"; the
  formula says 15. Over _N_ equal orders the error is _N_ ÷ (*N*−1). Against your Amazon
  history, **129 of the 217 repeat-purchase suggestions have exactly two orders** (2× too fast)

  days (and really it was an exchange, which no formula will see).

  Options: **(a)** divide by the units bought _before_ the last order —
  `(totalUnits − lastOrderUnits) ÷ span` — one more aggregate in

  evidence more loudly, since it is "an estimate the user is expected to correct"; **(c)**
