## Tried and backed out

- **Nothing was reverted.** Two lines of enquiry were dropped after failing to demonstrate
  a problem, which is the outcome the brief asks for rather than a fix on suspicion:

- **Paste order under concurrent server actions.** `useNodeCommandDeck.onPasteRows`
  (`:123`) computes the moves up front and then fires one `apply(...)` per row, each its
  own `startTransition`. Every move after the first is anchored to the previous row
  (`pasteMoves`, so "a block picked up in order arrives in that order"), which only holds
  if the actions are applied in order. I could not settle whether Next.js serialises
  Server Action dispatches from separate transitions, and testing it properly means moving
  real nodes between projects. Logged under _Needs a decision_ rather than guessed at.

- **Clicking an Outline row "not selecting".** Looked like a live bug for a while: `click`
  on a row below the fold left the selection where it was. It is the driver — the rows
  live in a scroll container, and `document.elementFromPoint` at the row's centre returns
  `null`, so the click lands on nothing. Not an app defect; noted so the next run does not
  chase it again.

## Needs a decision

- **Editing an appointment whose Google event was deleted elsewhere fails, permanently.**
  `pushUpdate` (`src/lib/google/writeThrough.ts:92`) patches Google inside the mutation and
  does not catch `GoogleEventGoneError`, which `googleFetch` throws on 404/410
  (`client.ts:115`). `pushDelete` right below it *does* catch it, deliberately. So: delete
  an event on your phone, edit the still-mirrored row here before the next mirror pass, and
  every save throws. Options: (a) leave it — the asymmetry is defensible, since a patch that
  cannot reach Google must not quietly succeed locally, and a reload drops the row;
  (b) catch `GoogleEventGoneError` in `pushUpdate` and delete the local row instead, on the
  grounds that Google is the source of truth and the row is provably stale; (c) catch it and
  surface "this event no longer exists in Google — refresh". **Recommendation:** (c). It is
  the honest message, and (b) throws away planner-only fields (three-state check, priority,
  contexts, project link) on what may be a transient 404.
  — blocking: nothing today; needs a linked account to hit at all.

- **A completed parent with an open, overdue child reads as Overdue.**
  `scheduleStatusById` (`src/lib/tree/status.ts:222`) propagates a child's `overdue` up, and
  `moreUrgent` ranks `overdue` above `completed` — so the row's State says Completed while
  its Status says Overdue. Reachable by completing a project and later reopening a child, or
  filing a new task under a finished one. Options: (a) stop propagation into a parent whose
  own local status is `completed`; (b) leave it, on the grounds that "marked done but has
  overdue work under it" is worth shouting about. **Recommendation:** (b) plus nothing —
  I think the current behaviour is right and the contradiction is the point, but it is a
  product call and I did not want to change a status rule unattended.
  — blocking: nothing.

- **The rail groups in insertion order; Metrics sorts its groups.** `ProjectsRail`'s Group
  by Result Area lists areas in whatever order the projects happen to come in, with
  `(No Result Area)` wherever it first appears; `MetricsView` sorts owner groups
  alphabetically with the ownerless one pinned first. One of the two is wrong and it is a
  taste call which. **Recommendation:** match Metrics — pinned "none" then alphabetical — it
  is the one a reader can predict.
  — blocking: nothing.
